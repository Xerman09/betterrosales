Gateway of the Ilocos Region

The Crossroads of Heritage and Progress

Strategically positioned at the end of TPLEX Phase 1 and the MacArthur Highway, Rosales is a 1st-class municipality spanning 37 barangays. Home to nearly 67,000 residents, it serves as the vibrant eastern gateway to the Ilocos Region. Whether you are craving the legendary tupig of Carmen, exploring centuries-old architecture, or investing in our rapidly growing commercial hubs, Rosales offers a perfect balance of rural charm and urban convenience.

Civic Leadership Archive

Mayors of Rosales, Pangasinan

Chronological transitions of municipal leadership and notable milestones from the post-war era to the present.

Modern and Recent Mayors (2000s to Present)
2022 – Present Incumbent

Hon. William “Liam” S. Cezar

The incumbent mayor of Rosales. First elected in 2022 and ran unopposed in the 2025 midterm elections, overseeing continuing commercial growth and infrastructure expansion along the TPLEX corridor.

2013 – 2022 3 Consecutive Terms

Hon. Susan P. Casareno

Served the maximum three consecutive terms, guiding Rosales through rapid modernization, business expansion including SM City Rosales, COVID-19 response, and Typhoon Pepeng flood recovery.

2004 – 2013 Presidencia Renovation

Hon. Ricardo V. Revita

A retired police major whose 2004 victory ended a 37-year political stronghold. Over three terms, he spearheaded the major renovation of the historic 1924 Presidencia and modernized agricultural and local infrastructure.

2001 – 2004 1st Female Mayor

Hon. Maria Julieta Yu

Elected at the turn of the millennium as the first female mayor of Rosales, succeeding long-time local chief executive Remigio Yu.

Historical Figures & The Post-War Era
approx. 1967 – 2001 Longest-Serving

Hon. Remigio Yu

One of the longest-serving local executives in Pangasinan history. He defended his 1971 victory in the landmark Supreme Court case Casibang vs. Aquino and governed through Martial Law and the 1986 EDSA Revolution.

Prior to 1954 Governor & DAR Secretary

Hon. Conrado F. Estrella Sr.

Began his public service as Mayor of Rosales, helping restore St. Anthony of Padua Parish after the 1946 tornado before serving as Pangasinan Governor (1954–1963) and Agrarian Reform Secretary/Minister (1971–1986).

Brief History of Rosales

1852

Rosales was formally founded as an independent municipality on November 28, 1852 by Royal Decree, named in honor of Don Antonio Rosales.

1853

Originally established within the jurisdiction of Nueva Ecija, the town was formally annexed to the province of Pangasinan due to thriving trade routes and river navigation.

1902

Organized under the American civil administration, rapidly developing as the primary agricultural and marketplace center connecting the central plains to northern Luzon.

1939

The completion of the historic Carmen Bridge spanning the Agno River established Rosales as the irreplaceable transport crossroads and gateway of Eastern Pangasinan.

Present

Flourishing as a premier 1st-Class Municipality and regional economic power, hosting major regional transport terminals, SM City Rosales, and 37 progressive barangays.

Gateway of Eastern Pangasinan

Strategically connecting the provinces of Pangasinan, Tarlac, and Nueva Ecija, Rosales serves as the vital junction for regional travel, commerce, and agriculture.

Modern Commercial Hub

Home to major commercial complexes, banking hubs, modern public markets, and premier health facilities serving over 67,000 residents and neighboring towns.

Poblacion Civic District
Featured Civic Heritage · Completed 1924

Rosales Presidencia (Municipal Hall)

An enduring symbol of local governance and classic Filipino-colonial architecture. Completed in 1924 and carefully renovated in the 2000s, it sits right across the town plaza and stadium as the focal point of the civic district (closed on weekends).

  • Classic 1924 Filipino-colonial civic architecture
  • Surrounded by the town plaza, stadium, and heritage monuments

Catholic Church 4.8
Zone II, Rosales (Diocese of Urdaneta)

St. Anthony of Padua Parish Church

The spiritual heart of Rosales. Originally built as a humble shed in 1915, it survived natural disasters—including the 1946 tornado—and stands as a deeply respected heritage site.

  • Classic colonial and post-war church architecture
  • Centrally located in Zone II, accessible from town proper
